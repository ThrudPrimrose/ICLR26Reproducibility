Where the calls actually go

The packet is a rounding error next to the language. Fortran loses more than a third of its judged calls to wrong answers; C arms lose under 12%. This is the effect worth chasing.

Coverage and yield

Kernels that never produce a submission are invisible in any speedup statistic. Fortran reaches barely two-thirds of the set. Note also that more submissions do not buy more speedup — qwen30b submits 2.4× as often as oss120b on C and lands at half the median.

Caveat: the speedup column is quantized, and disagrees with the timings

Every recorded speedup lies on a reciprocal grid of two-decimal values — 33.333, 25.0, 20.0, 16.667, 11.111. Above roughly 5× a single grid step is a 25–33% jump with no intermediate value available, so differences in that region are resolution, not signal. Between 31% and 59% of each arm's kernels sit there.

Separately, speedup is not consistent with the stored baseline_ns and native_ns: raw ratios of 35.27, 31.24 and 22.92 are recorded as 33.333, 25.0 and 20.0 respectively. The two columns do not come from the same measurement, and which one is authoritative is unresolved.

This does not threaten the null result — the paired tests are dominated by kernels near parity, where the grid is fine — but it does make every mean speedup unusable, and the medians for oss120b and kimi27 fall inside the coarse region.

Reading notes

Paired, not aggregate. The two legs of an ablation do not cover identical kernel sets, so an unpaired median compares different populations. On Fortran the unpaired medians read 1.099 against 1.496 and look like a win; paired on the 26 kernels both legs reached, the packet is behind 6–9. Only the paired figure is reported above.

Ties are excluded from the sign test, which is standard, but they are large here (33 of 102) and they are informative: on a third of kernels both legs land on precisely the same best speedup, usually 1.000 — the agent found nothing either way.

kimi27 is not part of any ablation. It appears for context only: a partial, still-running no-skills arm (22 of 40 kernels at time of collection) served through SGLang rather than vLLM. Its numbers are not comparable to the qwen and oss arms, which differ in model and serving stack at once.
